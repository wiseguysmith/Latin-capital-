CAPITALREADINESSFRAMEWORK
A pre-underwriting standard for private capital across Latin America
Foundational Design Document · Version 1.0 · July 2026

Purpose — Standardize how prepared a business is to enter institutional or private-capital review—without replacing underwriting, credit committees, legal opinions, valuation, or regulated decision-makers.

Initial geography — Costa Rica, Panama, and El Salvador, designed for modular expansion across Latin America.

Platform posture — The platform orchestrates data, documentation, diligence, matching, and workflow. It does not lend, custody funds, guarantee approval, or make a regulated credit decision.


Prepared as a product, policy, and systems blueprint

1  Executive Summary
The proposed Capital Readiness Framework (CRF) is a standardized pre-underwriting system that converts fragmented borrower information into a transparent, lender-readable readiness package. It is designed to improve the quality, speed, comparability, and auditability of opportunities entering private-capital review across Latin America.
The CRF deliberately separates readiness from creditworthiness. A company may be highly organized yet economically weak; another may be economically attractive but operationally unprepared. Lenders still determine repayment probability, loss severity, pricing, structure, suitability, and approval. The CRF determines whether the opportunity is sufficiently complete, reliable, controlled, and explainable to deserve efficient review.
The architecture combines globally recognized credit principles—cash-flow analysis, leverage and liquidity review, governance, legal diligence, KYC/KYB, beneficial ownership, collateral analysis, use-of-proceeds controls, ongoing monitoring, and model governance—with a proposed certification-style operating system.
Recommended product output — Each assessment produces five artifacts: (1) a 0–100 Capital Readiness Score, (2) an Evidence Confidence grade, (3) an External Risk Context profile, (4) a red-flag and conditions register, and (5) a prioritized remediation plan.

2  Research Synthesis: How Capital Providers Evaluate Borrowers
Across commercial banks, development finance institutions, private credit, venture debt, asset-based lending, revenue-based finance, family offices, fintech lenders, and credit-rating methodologies, the recurring analytical questions are remarkably consistent:
Who is the borrower, who ultimately owns and controls it, and are the people trustworthy and competent?
What is the primary source of repayment, and how resilient is it under downside conditions?
What is the full debt burden, maturity profile, covenant burden, and liquidity position?
Is the information reliable, timely, reconciled, and capable of ongoing reporting?
Is the borrower legally able to enter the transaction, and can security or guarantees be enforced?
What industry, country, currency, political, climate, and concentration risks can impair repayment?
What is the secondary source of repayment or recovery if the operating case fails?
Is the use of proceeds specific, economically rational, traceable, and aligned with repayment?
Can the lender monitor performance, detect deterioration early, and exercise remedies?
Global practice — Basel credit-risk principles emphasize a sound credit-granting process, credit administration, measurement, monitoring, and controls. FATF standards require risk-based customer due diligence and beneficial-ownership transparency. Asset-based and real-estate lending guidance demonstrates that collateral and transaction type require specialized underwriting modules. [R1–R6]

Capital provider

Implication for CRF
Commercial bank

Development bank / DFI / MDB
Commercial viability plus development impact, governance, environmental and social risk, procurement/integrity, additionality.
Add ESG/E&S screening, impact indicators, anti-corruption, and stakeholder-risk evidence.
Private credit fund
Downside protection, documentation, covenants, sponsor/management quality, cash conversion, recovery, speed.
Emphasize downside case, covenant capacity, security perfection, and lender-grade data room.
Venture debt
Runway, recurring revenue, growth quality, investor support, next financing, IP, cash controls.
Use startup module; avoid forcing EBITDA-based thresholds onto pre-profit firms.
Asset-based lender
Eligible collateral, advance rates, borrowing base, dilution, aging, controls, field exams.
Use collateral sub-ledgers, eligibility rules, appraisal/verification, and frequent reporting.
Revenue-based finance
Bank/payment-processor data, revenue stability, unit economics, seasonality, platform concentration.
Use high-frequency transaction data with clear consent and model-governance controls.
Family office
Principal trust, asset protection, strategic fit, bespoke structure, reputation, relationship.
Combine institutional evidence with an executive narrative and transparent exceptions.
Credit rating agency
Business risk, financial risk, liquidity, leverage, coverage, management/governance, event risk, recovery.
Borrow analytical structure but do not call CRF a rating or imply default probability.

3  Framework Architecture
The CRF has six layers. Separating these layers avoids false precision and makes the system adaptable to different lenders, countries, and financing products.
Layer
Function
Key output
1. Eligibility & Integrity Gates
Determine whether the borrower can enter the process responsibly.
Pass, fail, or enhanced-review status.
2. Core Readiness Score (80 points)
Assess borrower-level financial, operational, legal, governance, and compliance readiness.
Comparable core score across business and financing types.
3. Financing Module (20 points)
Assess purpose, structure, repayment logic, collateral/alternative support, and monitoring.
Deal-specific module score.
4. Evidence Confidence
Assess source quality, independence, reconciliation, coverage, and freshness.
Grade A–D and field-level provenance.
5. External Risk Context
Report industry, country, currency, political, inflation, supply-chain, and climate exposures.
Context profile and mitigants—not a hidden score penalty.
6. Red Flags, Conditions & Overrides
Surface issues requiring human decisions and document exceptions.
Auditable conditions register and reviewer sign-off.

3.2  Core and Module Separation
Core score — Measures how institutionally prepared the business is regardless of lender: reliable reporting, repayment evidence, management, controls, legal organization, and compliance.

Financing module — Measures whether this specific request is coherent: purpose, amount, sources and uses, structure, repayment schedule, downside case, collateral/guarantees, and monitoring package.

External context — Measures conditions the borrower may not control. The score evaluates whether those risks are identified and mitigated; the raw country/industry risk remains a separate lender input.

4.1  Control Maturity Scale
Level
Definition
Typical evidence
0 — Absent
No process, document, owner, or reliable evidence.
Missing or contradicted information.
1 — Informal
Some information exists, but it is ad hoc, incomplete, stale, or person-dependent.
Self-declaration, spreadsheets without reconciliation, unsigned drafts.
2 — Documented
Policy/process/document exists but is inconsistently implemented or only partially evidenced.

3 — Implemented

Category and total scores must be calculated deterministically. Narrative AI may explain results, but it may not alter formulas, thresholds, evidence status, caps, or overrides.

6  Financial Health Metric Library
No universal threshold rule — Ratios must be interpreted by industry, business model, accounting quality, currency, seasonality, maturity, and financing type. The CRF may use configurable lender benchmarks, but it should not pretend that one DSCR, current ratio, or leverage threshold is universally correct.

Metric
Definition
Why it matters / cautions
Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR)
Cash flow available for debt service ÷ scheduled principal and interest.
Measures capacity to pay debt from operating cash generation. Test historical, projected, and downside DSCR; define numerator precisely.
Fixed-Charge Coverage
Earnings/cash flow available for fixed charges ÷ interest, principal, leases, and other fixed obligations.
Useful where lease or other fixed-payment obligations are material.
Current Ratio
Current assets ÷ current liabilities.
Broad short-term liquidity indicator; may overstate liquidity if inventory or receivables are weak.
Quick Ratio
Cash + marketable securities + eligible receivables ÷ current liabilities.
Stricter liquidity test; still requires receivables-quality analysis.
EBITDA
Earnings before interest, tax, depreciation, and amortization.
Operating earnings proxy, not cash flow. Normalize owner pay and one-offs; account for capex, taxes, working capital, and leases.
Operating Cash Flow
Cash generated by operations after working-capital movements.
Closer to actual debt-servicing capacity than accounting earnings; analyze volatility and conversion from EBITDA.
Free Cash Flow
Operating cash flow less maintenance/required capital expenditure.
Indicates cash available after sustaining operations; definitions must be transparent.
Burn Rate
Average net cash consumed per month.
Critical for pre-profit companies; must be paired with runway and financing milestones.
Cash Runway
Unrestricted cash ÷ monthly net burn.
Shows time to cash exhaustion under current and downside conditions.
Working Capital
Current assets less current liabilities; also analyze operating working capital.
Identifies short-term funding needs and whether growth consumes or releases cash.
Cash Conversion Cycle
Days inventory + days sales outstanding − days payables outstanding.
Shows how long cash is tied up in operations and where financing pressure arises.
Gross Margin
Revenue less direct cost of goods/services ÷ revenue.
Measures unit-level economics and pricing/cost resilience.
EBITDA / Operating Margin
EBITDA or operating profit ÷ revenue.
Indicates operating profitability and scale efficiency; normalize carefully.
Net Margin
Net income ÷ revenue.
Captures full cost structure but can be distorted by financing, tax, and non-recurring items.
Revenue Stability
Trend, variance, seasonality, recurrence, contract length, churn, and cohort quality.
Stable, diversified revenue generally supports more reliable repayment forecasts.
Customer Concentration
Top customer / top five / top ten share of revenue or receivables.
A single loss or delayed payment can impair liquidity and repayment.
Channel / Platform Concentration
Share of revenue dependent on a marketplace, processor, distributor, or referral source.
Creates policy, pricing, suspension, and data-access dependency.
Debt / EBITDA
Gross or net debt ÷ normalized EBITDA.
Common leverage indicator for mature businesses; unsuitable when EBITDA is negative or structurally volatile.
Debt / Equity
Total debt ÷ equity.
Indicates creditor-versus-owner financing; book equity quality and revaluation matter.
Interest Coverage
EBIT or EBITDA ÷ cash interest expense.
Measures capacity to pay interest; ignores principal and may overstate cash capacity.
Loan-to-Value (LTV)
Loan balance ÷ supportable collateral value.
Recovery/structure metric, not a substitute for cash-flow repayment analysis.
Receivables Dilution
Credits, returns, disputes, offsets, and write-offs ÷ gross receivables.
Critical in ABL because nominal receivables may not be collectible at face value.

6.1  Required Financial Analyses
At least 24–36 months of historical monthly performance where available, plus year-to-date and comparable period.
Integrated P&L, balance sheet, and cash-flow forecast with assumptions and actual-versus-budget tracking.
Base, downside, and severe-but-plausible stress cases.
Debt schedule including lender, original amount, balance, currency, rate, maturity, amortization, collateral, covenants, and arrears.
Customer/revenue concentration, receivables aging, payables aging, inventory aging where relevant.
Normalized earnings bridge showing owner compensation, related-party transactions, one-offs, and non-recurring items.
Currency mismatch and rate sensitivity for debt and major cash flows.
Working-capital and cash-conversion analysis tied to the requested financing amount.

17.1  Integration Principles
API data should not automatically outrank signed or audited evidence; conflicts must create an exception.
Every material field must retain source, timestamp, consent basis, transformation history, and reviewer status.
Use least-privilege, tokenized credentials, rotation, audit logging, vendor-risk review, and data-retention controls.
Design for manual fallback because many Latin American registries and institutions will not provide reliable APIs.
Do not make commercial launch dependent on perfect open banking. A secure, verified-document workflow is the practical MVP.
Country coverage and lawful-use rights must be tested before naming an integration “supported.”

19  Regulatory Considerations for Latin America
This section is an implementation checklist, not a country legal opinion. Before launch, local counsel should produce a written regulatory-perimeter memorandum for each country, product, lender type, borrower type, and fee flow.
Question
Why it matters
Does platform activity constitute lending, arranging, brokerage, financial intermediation, securities placement, investment advice, crowdfunding, payments, trust/fiduciary, or credit-bureau activity?
Licensing, registration, conduct, disclosure, and supervision may apply even if the platform never funds or custodies money.
Who is the customer and who pays?
Success fees, application fees, lender subscriptions, and referral fees can alter characterization and conflicts.
Is the product a bilateral commercial loan, syndicated participation, note, receivables purchase, investment contract, fund interest, or security?
Legal classification determines regulators, solicitation rules, transfer restrictions, disclosures, and investor eligibility.
How are KYC/AML responsibilities allocated?
The platform, lender, law firm, fiduciary, and payment provider need a documented responsibility matrix; reliance rules differ.
Can borrower and UBO data be collected, screened, transferred cross-border, retained, and used for automated analysis?
Privacy, consent, data localization, security, breach response, and automated-decision rules may apply.
How are liens and guarantees created, perfected, prioritized, monitored, and enforced?
Security is only useful if legally valid and practically enforceable in the asset jurisdiction.
What taxes and FX rules apply?
Withholding, stamp/registration taxes, transfer pricing, thin capitalization, interest deductibility, exchange controls, and currency mismatch affect economics.
What consumer or SME protections apply?
The MVP should focus on business-purpose financing, but sole proprietors and microenterprises may receive protective treatment.

21  Risks and Limitations of Readiness Scoring
Risk
Required safeguard
Readiness is mistaken for creditworthiness
Prominent disclaimers; separate score, context, and lender decision; prohibit approval language.
False precision
Show category scores, evidence grade, ranges/conditions, and source data—not just one number.
Score gaming / document theater
Cross-reconcile source data, sample controls, require attestation, track changes and anomalies.
Bias / exclusion
Test outcomes; separate external risk from controllable readiness; provide remediation and appeal.
Poor or fraudulent data
Evidence confidence, independent verification, conflict rules, human investigation.
Stale score
Expiration, freshness rules, material-change duty, monitoring and automatic downgrade to stale.
Lender heterogeneity
Core standard plus lender overlays; never claim universal approval thresholds.
Model drift and AI hallucination
Deterministic scoring, validation, monitoring, source citations, restricted LLM role.
Regulatory perimeter creep
Country/product legal matrix, role controls, no custody/lending, partner licensing checks.
Certification liability
Careful name/claims, terms, scope, methodology, reliance limitations, insurance, and audit.
Conflicts from fees
Disclose fees; independent score governance; analysts not paid for score uplift; monitor overrides.
Cross-border privacy/security
Data minimization, lawful basis, transfer mechanisms, regional architecture, vendor controls, incident plan.
Over-standardization
Modular business/financing types, N/A taxonomy, expert review, periodic consultation.
Weak outcome data
Pilot with design lenders, use conservative claims, publish validation limits, recalibrate transparently.

API transaction data, registry extract, signed contract.
E3 — Independent professional evidence
Independent accountant, auditor, lawyer, appraiser, engineer, notary, regulator, or verified registry.
Audit/review report, legal opinion, licensed appraisal.

Evidence rule
Recommended policy
Materiality
Define quantitative and qualitative materiality by control and financing module.
Freshness
Identity/sanctions at assessment and closing; bank/financial data recent; appraisals and legal searches module-specific.
Contradiction
Any material conflict creates an exception; do not average conflicting values.
Translation
Preserve original; certified translation where legal reliance requires it; machine translation labeled.
Attestation
Authorized officer attests completeness, accuracy, and duty to disclose material changes.
Certification validity
Time-limited; expires or becomes stale on material change, missing update, or new red flag.
N/A use
Allowed only by controlled taxonomy; never used merely because evidence is missing.
Reallocation
Weights may be redistributed only within approved module rules and recorded.
Reviewer independence
High-risk gates and overrides require maker-checker or specialist approval.
Borrower visibility
Borrower sees category results, missing evidence, reason codes, conditions, and correction path.
